1What we're building

There's a real government website in Madhya Pradesh, mpedistrict.gov.in, where people apply for an income certificate (आय प्रमाण पत्र). It's a document you need for scholarships, reservations, welfare schemes — proof of how much your family earns.

For the hackathon, we rebuild that service — the website and the system behind it — so it solves the real problem instead of just looking nicer. All the data we use is fake/synthetic — no real people, no real Aadhaar. We'll say so openly in the app.

2The problem we're fixing

Here's the thing most people don't know:

  • By law (a 2010 MP act), the government must deliver this certificate in 3 working days.
  • If they're late, the responsible officer owes you ₹250 for every day of delay — and you can appeal.
  • The only document you need to give is a signed statement of your own income. Nothing else.

But in reality: the website never tells you this promise exists, and it can't keep it anyway — because every single application, even the obviously honest ones, is made to wait behind a manual field visit by an officer (the पटवारी, Patwari) to "verify" a document that was just the person's own word.

So the fix isn't "add a countdown timer." It's smarter than that:

Check the person's claim against records the government already has → instantly approve the clean cases → only send a human to the few suspicious ones (and tell them why) → and put that 3-day legal deadline front and center, so if it's ever missed, the appeal and the penalty happen automatically.

3How it works, simply

A citizen opens the app on a cheap phone, logs in with just phone + OTP (no passwords, no captcha), and fills a short form: their income, where it comes from, and what it's for.

Then the heart of the project — an engine — checks their answers against several mock government databases (family records, ration card, land records, past certificates). Based on what it finds, it makes one of four decisions, and every decision comes with a plain reason the citizen can read (in Hindi and English):

Auto-issue

Looks clean

Everything checks out. Certificate issued in seconds. Done.

Send to officer

Something to check

One mild red flag. Goes to a human — with the specific reason attached so they know exactly what to look at.

Needs a fix

Missing / stale info

e.g. their ID records are out of date. We tell them the exact fix, and the clock pauses until they're back.

Reject

Clear contradiction

e.g. claims low income but holds a "poor family" ration card. We show the contradiction; they can correct or appeal.

4The signature piece: the statutory clock

This is the thing we want the judges to remember. Not a spinner — a track of 3 segments, one per working day, each showing its date and the name of the officer who's holding the file right now. Segments fill as days pass. If it runs past day 3, it turns red and a penalty counter starts ticking in rupees.

5The 5 stories we'll demo

The whole app is built to tell these five short stories end-to-end:

1
Sunita, Indore · the clean case

Everything's honest. Certificate auto-issued in about 8 seconds. Shows the new baseline: fast.

2
Ramesh, Dewas · a soft flag

His land doesn't quite match his income, so it's sent to an officer — with the reason shown. Officer approves it within the deadline. Shows targeted human review.

3
Kamla, Sehore · the deadline is missed

An officer sits on it too long. The clock runs out → an appeal is auto-drafted and the ₹250/day penalty starts. Shows the promise being enforced.

4
Arjun, Bhopal · blocked, not abandoned

His ID records are stale, so we ask him to fix one thing (clock pauses). He fixes it, resubmits, and it auto-issues. Shows blocked ≠ ignored.

5
A scholarship portal · data, not paper

Another website asks "is this family under ₹2.5 lakh?" and gets a signed yes/no back — no PDF upload. Shows why treating the certificate as data matters.

6A few rules we're sticking to

  • Hindi is the default, English is a toggle. It's for real MP citizens.
  • Works on a ₹6,000 phone on a slow connection — small, fast, screen at least 360px wide, big tap targets, usable one-handed.
  • Nothing ever silently waits. Every step shows who's responsible, when, and what happens next.
  • Every automatic decision is explained in plain words — no black box.
  • All data is fake, and we have an honesty page that says exactly what's real, what's mocked, and which real government system each mock would replace.

7The tech, briefly

Next.js (App Router) + TypeScript, Tailwind for styling, a simple file-based database (Prisma + SQLite) so there's zero setup, Zod for checking inputs, and Vitest for testing the engine. The look is pinned already: deep institutional green, a "document that carries legal weight" feel, two fonts (Mukta + Noto Sans Devanagari) — the same colors you're reading right now.

That's the whole idea. The one-line version: the state already promises this certificate in 3 days and already owes ₹250/day if it's late — we're building the software that finally keeps that promise, and proves it.
